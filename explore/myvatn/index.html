Lake Mývatn, half an hour from Hótel Laugar

A shallow lake in a landscape still being made, 30 km east of the hotel: pseudo craters, lava castles, boiling mud and a geothermal lagoon for the evening. Allow a full day.

By road
30 km
Drive
30 min east
Time needed
A full day
Best
Any day; a breezy one if possible

Mývatn is thirty minutes east of Laugar on the ring road, with a loop of stops around the lake. Start with the pseudo craters at Skútustaðir on the south shore, then visit Dimmuborgir, a field of black lava towers and arches with marked walks of half an hour to two hours. Beside it stands Hverfjall, a tephra crater whose rim takes about an hour to walk around.

East of the lake, the ground changes. At Hverir, beneath the Námafjall ridge, the earth boils and steams in mud pots and vents. A little further on, the Krafla road climbs to the crater lake Víti. Back towards the lake, finish the afternoon at the geothermal baths above the shore.

The lake takes its name from its midges. They do not bite, but they swarm on still, warm days in June or July. A head net from the shop is money well spent, and a breeze clears them.

  • Pairs with DettifossThe road to Dettifoss leaves the ring road east of Mývatn, so the big falls can be the afternoon of a Mývatn day for those who like long days.
  • LunchLunch packs from the hotel on request, or the cafés at Reykjahlíð on the north shore.
  • HverfjallWith Vindbelgjarfjall, Astrid’s pick for the best view over the lake. Walk up to the crater rim for views over Mývatn, Dimmuborgir and the lava fields. Keep to the paths; the northwest approach is gentler than the steep southern one.
  • VindbelgjarfjallAnother walk for the lake views, starting at the car park west of Vagnbrekka farm. Follow the marked path to the summit; parts can be steep and slippery.
  • Krafla and VítiThe blue-green lake at Víti and the volcanic ground around it feel a world apart. Walk around the crater, or explore the geothermal area and craters at nearby Leirhnjúkur. Keep to the paths, and take care in wet weather, when the ground can be slippery.
  • On to HúsavíkFrom Reykjahlíð, take road 87, Kísilvegur, north, then join road 85 for Húsavík. Astrid calls it one of the most silent places on earth: a stone desert with hardly a bird, where on a still day the silence rings in your ears. In June the lupins turn it violet.

Questions

How far is Hotel Laugar from Lake Mývatn?

Lake Mývatn is 30 km from the hotel, about 30 minutes by car east along the ring road.

How long do I need at Mývatn?

A full day covers the pseudo craters, Dimmuborgir, Hverfjall, Hverir and a geothermal bath. Half a day covers two of them.

Are the midges at Mývatn bad?

On still warm days in June and July they swarm, though they do not bite. A head net, sold at the petrol stations, and a windy day solve it.
