Winter in North Iceland

Hótel Laugar closes after the summer, but Stella and Eyþór’s other two places stay open all year, under the northern lights.

The hotel in winter

Hótel Laugar is a summer hotel. For the rest of the year the three houses are the boarding school in Laugar, and the hotel opens again for the summer season.

What runs through the winter between Akureyri and Mývatn. Times change with the weather, so check with each operator before you go.

The northern lights

Late August to mid April

On dark nights with a reasonably clear sky. The Icelandic Met Office forecast shows aurora activity and cloud cover side by side, so you can choose your evening.

Huskies at Vallholt

Snow Dogs Iceland · about 13 km from Laugar

A family kennel of about thirty Siberian huskies near Lake Mývatn. In winter they run dog sledding tours and kennel visits.

Earth Lagoon Mývatn

Open daily, 12:00 to 22:00

The geothermal baths above Lake Mývatn, formerly the Mývatn Nature Baths, open through the winter with shorter hours over Christmas.

Earth Lagoon (opens in a new tab)

Skiing at Hlíðarfjall

Above Akureyri · around December to the end of April

The ski area on the mountain above Akureyri, a short drive from the town.

Whales in winter

From Akureyri and Dalvík

Tours from Akureyri and Dalvík sail all year. Húsavík’s North Sailing tours run from 1 March to 30 November.

Before you drive

  • DaylightAround three hours in Akureyri at the winter solstice, with sunrise just before noon. By early March there are about ten.
  • ColdAkureyri averages around −2 °C from December to February, with daytime highs just above freezing.
  • TyresStudded tyres are allowed from around the end of October to mid April, and whenever conditions require them.
  • RoadsCheck umferdin.is before you set off: the Road Administration’s live map of closures and conditions.
  • Tunnel tollVaðlaheiðargöng, east of Akureyri: pay at veggjald.is up to 24 hours before or after you drive through.

Sources: the Icelandic Met Office, timeanddate.com, umferdin.is, veggjald.is, the Icelandic police, North Sailing, Snow Dogs Iceland and Earth Lagoon, read October 2026.
